## Problem Statement
HW9 asks for a promo page for MarketLens, published on GitHub Pages from the public repository and due Monday 16 November 2026, 20:00. The page has to carry what the instructor said the 1 December demo misses: after 26 replay sessions the screen only says "Not enough history yet", so the audience never sees the real result (2,871 five-session calls, edge −2.9 points, no measurable difference) or what it means for a beginner investor.

## What Makes This Cool
- The headline is an honest negative result: "2,871 forecasts. No measurable edge." Most finance pages promise an edge; this one proves there was none and explains why that is worth knowing.
- The bar is the simplest guess, not a coin flip. In 2023–2026 "always up" was right 55.3% of the time, so a forecaster at 53% beats chance and still loses to doing nothing.
- The page ends with one sentence a beginner can use: ask any forecast for its record against the simplest guess before trusting it.

## Constraints
- Static page in `docs/` on GitHub Pages, no server; the app itself stays local.
- Nothing personal on the page: Pages publishes every file in `docs/`.
- Only measured numbers from `scripts/contenders_output.txt` (2023-01-03 to 2026-11-02, SPY, IEF, GLD), plus the series in `docs/promo-data.json` (see Recommended Approach, item 7).
- Wording uses the tool's own verdict ("no measurable difference"), never "the model lost"; states the period; says it counts correct calls, not profit.
- The look comes from Impeccable (shape, generate, critique) in HW9 step 3; this document fixes content and order only.
- Visual direction requested by the user: a light, lavender and white, card-based dashboard look with large numbers and small sparkline charts and a friendly hero illustration (reference image supplied in the session, a commercial crypto dashboard template). Take the style only, not the template's name, logo or artwork. Generate alternatives to compare against it.

## Recommended Approach
Approach A, chosen by the user. Content in this order (wording from the approved-for-review wireframe):

1. **Hero.** "2,871 forecasts. No measurable edge." / "MarketLens scores every forecast against the simplest guess: "the market goes up". Its own trained model could not beat it." / small print: "SPY, IEF and GLD, 2023 to 2026, five trading sessions ahead. Counts correct calls, not profit."
2. **Scoreboard** (same observations for every contender): Always up 2,871 calls, 55.3% right; My trained model (calibrated logistic) 52.4%; 20-session momentum 51.0%. "Edge of my model over always up: −2.9 points (approximate 95% range −6.9 to +1.1). Verdict: no measurable difference." Next-session price: model error $2.27, repeating the last close $2.27, also no measurable difference.
3. **Why you can trust the number** (what the measurement itself guarantees): Only the past (each contender sees sessions up to the day of its call, never after); Every call counts (all 2,871 five-session calls are scored, none dropped); Same test for all (same days and assets for every contender). One line attributes the record-keeping to the app: "In the app, every forecast is stored before its outcome exists and is never rewritten; the measurement issues exactly the app's forecasts, checked call by call on a short replay (tests/test_contenders.py), and on the original 2023–2024 replay both give the same 52.31% on 1,491 calls (scripts/spike_replay_output.txt). The full 2023–2026 run has not been compared with the app."
4. **The real screen:** `docs/model-history-contenders.png`. Caption describes what it actually shows: "The app's Model history after 26 replay sessions (66 calls, January to February 2023). Here the model ties always up at 59.1%, and MarketLens says 'Not enough history yet (22 of 30 shared sessions)': it refuses a verdict on a short record. Over all 2,871 calls the picture is the scoreboard above."
5. **What it means for a beginner:** "A forecast is worth nothing until it has a record against the simplest guess. Before you trust one, ask for that record."
6. **Footer:** run it yourself (GitHub repository, Python 3.13, offline on 1,213 real sessions); course project; free Yahoo Finance daily closes; educational, not investment advice; paper money only.
7. **Sparkline data (only if the chosen look uses small charts):** a committed script `scripts/export_promo_data.py` runs the same replay as `scripts/contenders.py` and writes `docs/promo-data.json` with the scoreboard, the edge and the 20-session rolling accuracy from `rolling()` for always up, momentum and the model. Small charts plot only that series; with no small charts the file is not needed. Any shape without data is decoration, never a chart.
